VAR-Q: Tuning-free KV Cache Quantization for Visual Autoregressive Image and Video Generation

Boxun Xu1,†Jiaji Lu1,†Yuxuan Yin1Zihu Wang1Ziyue Liu1Yu Wang1Zirui Liu2Peng Li1

1 University of California, Santa Barbara2 University of Minnesota

† Equal contribution · Correspondence: boxunxu@ucsb.edu

Paper ↗Code ↗BibTeX

VAR-Q quantizes the key/value cache of visual autoregressive models to 2–8 bits with no calibration data and no retraining. It assigns one scale factor per feature channel and per generation block (a scale or a frame), following the measured magnitude structure of the cache. Across five model families and nine checkpoints, 4-bit KV caches reduce cache memory by 72–75% at generation quality close to BF16; Infinity-8B keeps its composition at INT2.

Next-block prediction: one view of next-scale and next-frame generation

Visual autoregression is next-block prediction. A block is the set of visual tokens emitted at one decoding step: a resolution scale in next-scale models (VAR, Infinity, InfinityStar), a frame in next-frame models (Self-Forcing, LongLive). Every block attends to the KV cache of all blocks before it.

Seen this way, both paradigms leave behind the same object: a KV cache indexed by feature channel and generation block. Its magnitudes share a channel × block structure: a small set of channels carries large values, and their magnitudes shift from block to block.

Why it matters for quantization. VAR-Q assigns one scale factor per channel within each block, reusing the block boundaries the generation loop already provides. No outlier detection, no calibration data, no retraining.

Origin of the channel × block structure

Why does the channel × block pattern emerge? Three experiments separate prompt-dependent outliers, persistent channel imbalance, and position-dependent magnitude shifts. Together they explain why one grouping rule, channel × block, transfers across every model family in the paper.

01 / Architecture or input?

Outlier channels persist across prompts in K, far less in V

If outlier channels are tied to the architecture, they should recur across different prompts. Among the top 1% of channels, 53–62% of K outliers persist across all 10 prompts, compared with only 7–14% for V.

This supports a persistent architectural component in K. V outlier identity changes much more with the input. The remaining experiments trace this architectural component through the K projection chain.

02 / The channel axis

The K projection chain predicts outlier channels

Projection weights seed the imbalance. Normalization gains can amplify it. RoPE redistributes it between paired channels. Each stage contributes to predicting which channels become outliers.

WK

Seed

Heavy-tailed row norms in the key projection create an initial channel-magnitude imbalance.

→
γK

Amplify

Where post-projection RMSNorm is present, its learned channel gains amplify this imbalance.

→
RoPE

Redistribute

Rotation scatters magnitude between paired channels while preserving each pair’s ℓ2 norm.

The evidence

At the top 1%, WK alone recovers 35–42% of final K outliers. Adding γK improves recall; including RoPE scattering brings the full chain to 55–63× random recall.

As a control, WV predicts V outliers at only 5–6× random recall. Weight structure explains much more of K’s outlier identity than V’s.

03 / The block axis

RoPE redistributes magnitude across blocks

RoPE’s angle changes with position. For a paired set of K channels, this produces cosine and sine modulation: one channel can become large while its partner becomes small, even without a change in the underlying input amplitude.

|K2i| = M |cos θ||K2i+1| = M |sin θ|θ = bωi + φ · b is the block index

The pair’s per-token ℓ2 norm is conserved. Individual channel magnitudes are not. Fast-rotating pairs produce stronger cross-block swings; slow pairs remain comparatively stable.

Channel × block KV-cache quantization

A channel × block view of the KV cache
explains where quantization groups belong.

01

Quantization sensitivity is concentrated in outlier channels

Masking just the top 0.5% of channels in LongLive collapses generation. Masking ten times as many inlier channels leaves quality largely intact.

02

Magnitude structure spans channels and generation blocks

Large magnitudes persist along feature channels and shift across generation blocks. Grouping along only one axis misses part of the structure.

03

Scale- and frame-aligned quantization

A scale or frame defines a block. VAR-Q reuses these boundaries, with no outlier detection, calibration data, or retraining.

Quantization, bit packing, and retrieval

VAR-Q quantizes the cache per channel and block, then packs low-bit values into INT32 storage. Before attention, cached values are unpacked and dequantized to BF16. Packing turns reduced precision into real KV-memory savings.
